Review Wikipedia: Email Spoofing

I found the information in the Email Spoofing activity very interesting and informative. I already knew about online fraud, phishing, and similar dangers, but this activity gave me a deeper understanding of the topic. I learned that email spoofing is a technique in which the sender’s address displayed in the “From” field is modified or forged. As a result, an email may appear trustworthy and seem to come from a familiar person, company, or organization, even though it was actually sent by someone else. This technique can be used for different types of scams, including phishing, information theft, and the distribution of malware. The article also describes serious incidents involving large companies and organizations between 2013 and 2017. I also learned about protection methods such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, although these technologies cannot completely eliminate the risk. I can apply this knowledge in both my personal and professional life by being cautious with unexpected emails, checking the sender’s address, and avoiding links or attachments in suspicious messages.

Review Google: Message Headers

For this activity, I decided to analyze an email that I received in my Outlook account because that is where I receive a large amount of spam. I selected an unwanted message that seemed suspicious to me. I opened the message details, copied the full message header, and pasted it into the Google Admin Toolbox Messageheader website. The tool analyzed the header and displayed information about the sender, the servers used to deliver the message, the delivery time, and the results of the SPF, DKIM, and DMARC authentication checks. I learned that the full header can be used to trace the technical origin and route of an email. The message passed all three authentication checks, meaning that it was sent by servers authorized to use that domain and was probably not a typical case of email spoofing. However, passing these checks does not guarantee that the content or offer is trustworthy. In the future, I can use this tool to investigate suspicious messages before opening links, downloading attachments, responding, or providing personal information.
